# Polyglot Community Code of Conduct

Polyglot brings together people across languages, cultures, ages, abilities, beliefs, and levels of expertise. Participation requires dignity, patience, intellectual honesty, and respect for the communities whose languages are being studied.

## Our commitments

Participants are expected to:

- welcome learners and contributors without ridicule for accent, fluency, literacy, disability, or educational background;
- discuss corrections with evidence and kindness;
- distinguish disagreement from personal attack;
- respect names, pronouns, scripts, dialects, and community self-identification;
- credit sources, translators, speakers, artists, and cultural knowledge holders;
- protect children, students, and vulnerable participants from unwanted contact or disclosure;
- disclose conflicts of interest and meaningful AI assistance;
- accept moderator direction; and
- make space for people directly connected to the language or community under discussion.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not permitted:

- harassment, threats, stalking, intimidation, or targeted humiliation;
- racism, xenophobia, antisemitism, Islamophobia, caste discrimination, sexism, homophobia, transphobia, ableism, or other demeaning conduct;
- mocking an accent, dialect, literacy level, name, or non-native speaker;
- sexualized conduct or content directed toward participants;
- publishing private information without permission;
- pressuring students or minors into private communication;
- impersonating an educator, community representative, or Academy official;
- deliberate misinformation, fabricated citations, or knowingly false translations;
- unauthorized commercial solicitation or spam;
- destructive technical activity; and
- retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith.

## Scholarly disagreement

Languages contain variation, uncertainty, contested histories, and changing usage. Strong disagreement is allowed when it remains focused on evidence and ideas.

When multiple forms are valid, contributors should label region, dialect, register, historical period, or community preference rather than declaring one universal form without support.

## Scope

This code applies in repository issues, pull requests, discussions, Academy-linked collaboration spaces, public events representing Polyglot, and private communication that directly affects participation in the project.

## Reporting

Conduct concerns may be reported privately to:

**KhaemenesAcademy@protonmail.com**  
**Suggested subject:** `Private Polyglot Conduct Report`

Include the relevant dates, locations, links, participants, and a factual description. Do not forward sensitive student information more widely than necessary.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take proportionate action, including:

1. a private clarification or correction request;
2. removal or editing of harmful content;
3. a formal warning;
4. temporary limits on participation;
5. rejection of a contribution;
6. removal from a project space; or
7. referral to the relevant platform or authority when safety or law requires it.

Reports will be handled with as much privacy and fairness as reasonably possible. Absolute confidentiality cannot be promised when action is needed to protect a person or comply with legal obligations.

## Good-faith mistakes

Language learning includes mistakes. The purpose of this code is not to punish ordinary errors made in good faith. Participants should be willing to learn, correct harmful wording, and improve their work.
